## (a) Can a phone-based face/iris/palm capture be trusted against deepfakes and camera injection?

### Takeaway
No, not on its own. Vendor data for 2024–2025 shows very fast growth in injection attacks (virtual cameras, emulators, iOS injection) and in face-swap deepfakes. The main certification people cite, ISO/IEC 30107-3 (iBeta "Level 1/2"), explicitly leaves out injection: it only covers attacks presented to the sensor. Injection now has its own specification, CEN/TS 18099 (approved Nov 2024, national adoptions in 2025). NIST's only large independent PAD test (2023) found no algorithm that detected every attack type. Open-source PAD models only handle simple physical spoofs. A phone capture is therefore at best one signal. On its own it cannot establish uniqueness.

**Device attestation [mostly official documentation]**

### Inferences
- Because ISO 30107-3 excludes injection, an "iBeta Level 2" badge says nothing about virtual-camera or emulator attacks. Injection resistance needs a separate test against CEN/TS 18099 (labs such as Ingenium and BixeLab advertise this). A vendor that cites only 30107-3 covers half the threat model.
- Device attestation binds a capture to "real, unmodified hardware plus the genuine app". It does not bind it to "this person, now, in this place". Device farms with real devices pass. Hooking and replay at runtime remain open on Android (SBC 2025). For an app distributed outside the stores, Key Attestation (Android) remains usable; the app-identity part of Play Integrity does not. iOS has no comparable route outside the App Store.
- A secure but realistic design combines hardware attestation, server-side challenge-response (nonce in the capture, short TTL), and treats biometrics only as a deduplication signal. The physical-presence component (part b) then carries the Sybil resistance.

## (b) Which technologies can prove physical co-presence of a group of phones/people (incl. attacks, SDKs), and how do randomness, ceremony designs and human face recognition play in?

### Takeaway
No common phone radio technology delivers a relay-proof proof of proximity. BLE is relayable by specification (Bluetooth SIG 4.0–5.3 makes no relay-resistance claim). UWB 802.15.4z HRP was designed for secure ranging but has practical distance-reduction attacks (Ghost Peak up to 4% success; Mix-Down up to 100% on some chips). Countermeasures such as random time hopping exist so far only as a proposal. Witness-based location proofs (APPLAUS, MobChain, ProLoc) all struggle with collusion between prover and witnesses. The strongest practical Sybil barrier remains the Encointer approach: a globally simultaneous ceremony, randomized small groups at random places, and mutual human attestation. It still cannot prevent a person from attending with two devices if the group colludes, and it is weak in video form. drand quicknet gives a publicly verifiable, unbiasable randomness source for group assignment.

### Cited Findings
**Bluetooth LE**
- Staat, Jansen, Zenger, Elders-Boll, Paar, "Analog Physical-Layer Relay Attacks with Application to Bluetooth and Phase-Based Ranging" (arXiv 2202.06554, WiSec '22). A cheap off-the-shelf analog relay manipulates multi-carrier phase-based ranging (MCPR) arbitrarily while relaying over 90 m. Demonstrated against a car's BLE access and a smart lock. — [arXiv 2202.06554](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06554v2); [CASA Bochum PDF](https://casa.rub.de/fileadmin/img/Publikationen_PDFs/2022_Analog_Physical-Layer_Relay_Attacks_with_Application_to_Bluetooth_and_Phase-Based_Ranging_Publication_ClusterofExcellence_CASA_Bochum.pdf)
- NCC Group advisory (May 2022): Bluetooth Core 4.0–5.3 affected. An attacker can fake BLE proximity by relaying, and the Core spec makes no relay-resistance claim. — [NCC Group](https://www.nccgroup.com/research/technical-advisory-ble-proximity-authentication-vulnerable-to-relay-attacks/)

**UWB (IEEE 802.15.4z HRP)**
- Ghost Peak (Leu, Camurati, Heinrich, Roeschlin, Anliker, Hollick, Čapkun, Classen; USENIX Security 2022): first over-the-air attack on HRP UWB. Reduces 12 m to 0 m between Apple U1 chips (iPhone/AirTag) and also with NXP and Qorvo. Success up to 4%, hardware about USD 65, no key material needed. The authors question HRP for security-critical uses. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity22/presentation/leu); [arXiv 2111.05313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05313)
- "Time for Change: How Clocks Break UWB Secure Ranging" (Anliker, Camurati, Čapkun; USENIX Security 2023). Mix-Down exploits clock-drift compensation in SS-TWR: 10 m to 0 m on commercial chips, success up to 100% depending on reply time, message content unchanged, and per the slides no simple countermeasure. Stretch-and-Advance against the 802.15.4ab draft: analytically more than 90 m reduction, conceptual so far. The paper proposes a DS-TWR countermeasure for 802.15.4ab. — [USENIX 2023](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/anliker); [arXiv 2305.09433](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.09433); [Slides](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec23_slides_anliker.pdf)
- Defence by random time hopping (Gou, Yu, Wu, UESTC; arXiv 2406.06252; v2 titled "Resilient Random Time-hopping Reply against Distance Attacks in UWB Ranging"): time-hopping values are pre-stored as a hash table and chosen at random, with no extra signalling and backward-compatible with 802.15.4a/z. Claimed to cut Ghost-Peak-style success below 0.01%. This is a preprint with no independent replication. — [arXiv 2406.06252](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06252v2)
- Also cited: the UWBAD jamming attack (2024). — [arXiv 2407.00682](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00682v1)

**UWB on phones / APIs**
- Android: Jetpack androidx.core.uwb, stable 1.0.0, 1.1.0-alpha02 as of 09 Sep 2026. isAvailable() (alpha09) checks support. Third-party apps can set ranging interval, slot length and AoA (alpha08). Provisioned STS is supported where the device supports it (alpha07). Without GMS it falls back to an AOSP UWB backend. — [Android Developers: core-uwb releases](https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/core-uwb)
- Apple Nearby Interaction: distance and direction between opted-in devices with a U1 chip (from iPhone 11, iOS 14). Peers exchange NIDiscoveryToken (random per session) out of band, with one NISession per peer. EDM peer sessions from iOS 17. Third-party accessories need MFi UWB chipsets. Check with NISession.isSupported. — [WWDC20 "Meet Nearby Interaction"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10668/); [Apple docs: extending direction finding](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/nearbyinteraction/extending-advanced-direction-finding-and-ranging.md); [Dev Forums 661148](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/661148)

**Acoustic / ultrasound / context copresence**
- Distance bounding is the classic relay defence but needs precise round-trip timing low in the stack and often special hardware. Using ultrasound directly for distance bounding is itself relayable. Sound stays mostly within a room. — [IPDPS 2007 ultrasound paper](https://www.cecs.uci.edu/~papers/ipdps07/pdfs/SSN-1569015921-paper-1.pdf)
- DoubleEcho (arXiv 1803.07211) checks copresence through room impulse responses and addresses context-manipulation attacks, where the attacker plays the same signal at both locations. — [arXiv 1803.07211](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.07211)
- Contact Tracing Made Un-relay-able (arXiv 2010.12641) is relevant to BLE relays in a contact context (not read in detail). — [arXiv 2010.12641](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12641)

**Location proofs with witnesses**
- APPLAUS (Penn State, INFOCOM 2011): nearby BLE devices sign location proofs for each other, which go to a central server, with rotating pseudonyms. Criticism: the central architecture lets administrators track pseudonyms. — [Penn State](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/applaus-a-privacy-preserving-location-proof-updating-system-for-l/); [MDPI Sensors 21(15):5096](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/21/15/5096)
- MobChain (Zafar et al., 2020): targets three-way collusion between prover and witnesses. Witnesses and location authorities are chosen by distributed consensus over a private blockchain. Prototype only. — [arXiv 2011.08538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08538)
- ProLoc (De Viti, Ingo, Sheff, Druschel, Garg; MPI-SWS; arXiv 2404.04297, Apr 2024): "region proofs" (within distance d of a location), BLE advertisement exchange, retroactive ("in hindsight") proofs so the attacker cannot know in advance which location to fake. Collusion defence by a TrustRank variant over self-reported trajectories and encounters, with no extra infrastructure. — [arXiv 2404.04297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04297)
- FOAM: radio beacons with zone-based clock synchronisation by consensus. Sources are thin. — [Survey arXiv 2508.14230 (Cybernetica 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14230v1); [Blockchain-based Proof of Location arXiv 1607.00174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.00174)
- The 2025 taxonomy of proof-of-location systems (Cybernetica, arXiv 2508.14230) places APPLAUS among early distributed proposals. — [arXiv 2508.14230](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14230v1)

**Randomness beacons**
- drand quicknet (League of Entropy): GA since Oct 2023, a round every 3 s, "unchained" randomness, scheme ID bls-unchained-g1-rfc9380 (RFC 9380 hash-to-curve compliant). Each round maps to a fixed time, which makes it suitable for timelock encryption (tlock, threshold BLS plus Boneh–Franklin IBE). — [drand: quicknet is live](https://drand.love/blog/2023/10/16/quicknet-is-live); [drand: fastnet sunset](https://docs.drand.love/blog/2023/07/03/fastnet-sunset-quicknet-new); [tlock paper ePrint 2023/189](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/189); [Timelock audit](https://drand.love/press/audit-of-drand-time-lock-encryption)
- Third-party use: MegaETH uses quicknet as a VRF/randomness source. — [MegaETH docs](https://docs.megaeth.com/developer-docs/vrf)
- Libraries: drand-rs (Rust), drand/tlock (Go). — [drand-rs](https://www.github.com/thibmeu/drand-rs)

**Encointer ceremony (secondary sources; book.encointer.org not directly reachable)**
- Phase cycle: Registering (participants, communities, locations), Assigning (query meetup assignments), Attesting (perform meetups). — [Encointer Book: CLI](https://book.encointer.org/tutorials-cli.html); [Encointer Book: Protocol](https://book.encointer.org/protocol.html)
- Original design (Brenzikofer, arXiv 1912.12141, 2019): registration at least 24 h beforehand. The chain assigns randomized groups of 3–12 people to a random meetup location at a fixed time. The ceremony takes place "at high sun on the same day all over the world", so nobody can attend two meetups. Attestation was by pairwise key-signing in the app, with a witnessing window up to start + 24 h. — [arXiv 1912.12141](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.12141v1)
- Current app flow: the group first votes on the number of people present (a human check), and a latecomer forces a restart. Then each person shows a QR code that the others scan and sign. — [Encointer Book: Perform a Ceremony Meetup](https://book.encointer.org/app-meetup.html); [Encointer App page](https://encointer.org/encointer-app/index.html)
- Reputation: attending at least one meetup makes someone "reputable", and a registered no-show is demoted to newbie. — [Encointer: Charting the development…](https://encointer.org/charting-the-development-of-encointers-longest-established-community-currency/)
- Scalability thesis (ETH 2021): the newbie share should stay below 25% per meetup. — [ETH MA-2021-42](https://pub.tik.ee.ethz.ch/students/2021-HS/MA-2021-42.pdf)
- Security analysis (Hoffmann 2021, EPFL/Encointer): criteria include randomized meetup locations, and reputable participants should have at least one valid attestation. — [Hoffmann report PDF](https://encointer.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/report-2021-1-hoffmann_21_pop_security_encointer.pdf)
- BeeDance (proposal): an event host announces location and time range on-chain and signposts spots for about 10 people each to attest mutually. — [Introducing BeeDance](https://encointer.org/introducing-beedance/); [HackMD Brenzikofer](https://hackmd.io/@brenzi5/Sy6YtGAI3)
- EPFL DEDIS report "group PoP" (2020) covers related pseudonym-party designs. — [EPFL DEDIS report](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/dedis/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/report-2020-3-group-pop.pdf)

**Online/video ceremonies (comparison case Idena)**
- Idena: synchronous worldwide "validation ceremonies" (about every 2 weeks) with flip tests. The short time window is meant to stop one person validating several accounts. Invitation codes are capped, and the core team can issue invites. Criticism: excludes non-technical users and people with disabilities, and AI may solve CAPTCHA-style tests. — [Idena Medium](https://medium.com/idena/how-to-prove-your-identity-anonymously-919bdfe5249a); [Idena AI challenge](https://medium.com/idena/idena-blockchain-will-pay-25-000-for-solving-ai-hard-captchas-with-ai-39ff65f8ddc1); [ethresear.ch thread](https://ethresear.ch/t/can-anyone-come-up-with-attacks-for-idena/6260); [Ohlhaver, Harvard Ash Center 2024](https://ash.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/proof-of-personhood_ohlhaver.pdf)
- Bryan Ford (2025) on coercion resistance in personhood ceremonies. — [bford.info talk](https://bford.info/talk/2025-01-06-coercion-resistance/coercion-resistance.pdf)

**Human face recognition**
- GFMT (Burton, White, McNeill 2010, Behavior Research Methods 42(1):286–291): 168 pairs in the full version, 40 in the short version (Wikipedia says 164; discrepancy noted). Photos taken the same day with different cameras. The distribution implies half the participants make at least 8% errors, so the median is around 92% on the full version. — [DOI 10.3758/BRM.42.1.286](https://www.doi.org/10.3758/BRM.42.1.286); [Glasgow eprints](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/32220)
- Police super-recognisers average about 95.8% on the GFMT (NIST presentation, figure axes unclear). — [NIST Phillips IBPC 2016](https://www.nist.gov/document/11phillipsibpc2016facialforesnictestv1pdf)
- Super-recognisers and forensic examiners reach similar accuracy by different routes. Super-recognisers are fast, lean towards "same person", and make errors with high confidence. — [Sci Rep 2023 s41598-023-28632-x](https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-28632-x)
- Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 2025: super-recognisers do better on face perception, matching and memory. — [PMC12627160](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12627160/)
- Familiar faces are matched almost without error; unfamiliar faces are matched much worse. Live person against a photo (Kemp et al. 1997, cashiers and credit-card photos) is as error-prone as photo against photo. Bruce et al. 1999 used arrays of 1 target plus 10 others. — [White et al. 2014](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/95805/1/WKJMB_2014.pdf); [Matching and predictions (White Rose)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/86143/1/Matching_and_predictions_R2.pdf); [Megreya 2008 (gwern)](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/vision/2008-megreya.pdf)
- GFMT2 (psychometric successor version). — [Macquarie GFMT2](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/gfmt2-a-psychometric-measure-of-face-matching-ability/)

### Inferences
- **Proximity radio as a tie-breaker only:** BLE proximity is worthless against relays. UWB raises the effort considerably (special hardware, sub-ns timing, low success rates with Ghost Peak) but is not proof against chips vulnerable to Mix-Down. UWB is also missing on many cheaper Android phones. A protocol should treat UWB/BLE as an extra signal and never as the only presence proof.
- **Collusion is the core problem of every witness system:** a relay is unnecessary if the witnesses work with the prover. Defences rely on randomly chosen witnesses (MobChain), graph trust (ProLoc), or random groups at random places and times (Encointer). The common principle: the adversary must not be able to pick or predict the group or the place, so drand quicknet for assignment, combined with a commit-reveal of registrations before the beacon round.
- **Remote participation in the Encointer model** is prevented less by technology than by the combination: random location, global simultaneity (one place per person per ceremony), human head count, and mutual signatures. The remaining attack is a whole group colluding (all participants Sybils or bribed). The newbie quota (<25%) and random mixing limit it statistically.
- **Video meetups:** one person with two devices (two video tiles, or a deepfake face swap with a virtual camera, see part (a)) is hard to stop. Simultaneity across several randomized calls helps only if every person must be visible in exactly one call at the same time and the calls are randomly composed. Given the vendor data (face swap +300%, virtual cameras +2,665%), video is clearly weaker than physical meetings.
- **"People recognise people":** with unfamiliar faces, humans make errors in roughly 1 of 10 or more GFMT decisions even under ideal conditions (same-day photos). A meetup design should therefore not rely on strangers recognising a face again, but on simultaneous physical presence (counting heads) and possibly familiar participants (local community). Familiar faces are recognised almost without error.
